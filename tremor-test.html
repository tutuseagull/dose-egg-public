Tremor test

A working instrument, not a mockup. Twenty seconds of accelerometer, resampled, windowed, FFT, and the power that lands in the 3 to 7 Hz band where Parkinsonian resting tremor lives. No plugin, no entitlement, no watch. Open this on a phone.

The design decision this is testing. Not "can the app detect tremor and ask about it" — that inverts the relationship and interrupts people because they are unwell. This is the other direction: the person says they are shaking, and the phone offers to corroborate it. The question here is only whether the signal is real enough to be worth offering.

State 3 shows no number, and that is deliberate. A tremor score in the app is a measurement that invites a verdict, and the person can already feel their own hand. The number goes to the doctor artifact, where a measurement that is not self-reported is exactly what makes the record credible. If we ever surface it to the patient, the argument has to be made separately and it has to be better than “we had the number anyway.”
State 4 is the quality gate, and it matters more than it looks. cloudUPDRS is the only tool in the market research that scores its own trials for reliability and discards the bad ones rather than analysing them. Every consumer app treats all user input as equally valid. A brain-fogged evening or a moving car produces genuinely bad data, and a reading we know is unreliable is worse than no reading in a record a neurologist will read.

How it works, and the three things that will bite

  1. Sample timing is not uniform. devicemotion fires best effort and the interval drifts, so the raw series is resampled onto a fixed 50 Hz grid by linear interpolation before anything else happens. Skip this and the frequency axis is meaningless. This is the single most common mistake in web accelerometer code.
  2. Do not use vector magnitude. Taking sqrt(x²+y²+z²) feels natural and is wrong: rectifying a single-axis oscillation doubles its apparent frequency, so a 5 Hz tremor reads as 10 Hz. This computes a spectrum per axis and sums the three, which is rotation invariant and preserves frequency.
  3. Posture is part of the instrument. Resting tremor means resting. "Hold it in your lap, do not grip" is not friendly copy, it is the measurement protocol, and it is exactly the kind of instruction that gets followed differently on a bad day.

Chain: collect at whatever rate iOS gives, prefer e.acceleration (gravity already removed) and fall back to accelerationIncludingGravity with the mean subtracted, resample to 50 Hz, take 1024 samples (20.5 s, bin width 0.049 Hz), detrend, Hann window, radix-2 FFT per axis, sum the power spectra, then read the peak in 2 to 12 Hz and the fraction of power inside 3 to 7 Hz.

What a result does and does not mean. A clean 3 to 7 Hz peak is consistent with Parkinsonian resting tremor. It is not a diagnosis, it does not grade severity, and it cannot tell essential tremor from PD tremor. What it is good for is being an objective timestamp next to a subjective one, which is the thing a twelve minute appointment does not otherwise have.

What to try

  • Baseline. Rest the phone in your lap, hand relaxed, and run it. This is what "no tremor" looks like.
  • Deliberate. Shake your hand gently at roughly five times a second. The peak should land near 5 Hz. That validates the whole pipeline without needing a tremor.
  • The failure case. Run it while walking, or scrolling with your other thumb. This is what the quality gate has to catch.
  • The real question for P01. Not "does it work" but would you take this on a bad day. If the answer is no, it is a good days only instrument and belongs in the export or nowhere.
