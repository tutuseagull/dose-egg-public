E-01, computed

The first Explain-era statement, generated from a record rather than written by hand. The point is not the sentence — it is whether the machinery around it can be trusted to stay quiet when the record does not support speech.

Why this one. E-01 is the cross-dimension statement: lunch against the midday dose, against rough afternoons. Nothing else in the benchmark ships anything like it, and until now we had the sentence in the statement bank and the picture in a test page, with no connection between them. This is the join.
What Look Back would say

The gate, shown working

Every condition E-01 has to clear before it is allowed to speak. A generator that always speaks is a horoscope.

What the machinery does

One dot is one day

Two columns — lunch close to the midday dose, lunch further away — and every day in the record is a dot in one of them. A filled dot is a day with a rough afternoon; a hollow one is a day accounted for and ordinary. You count dots; you never measure a bar. Same reasoning as the protein blocks: at this size, counting survives what measuring does not.

Both columns are drawn at the same scale and both denominators are stated in the sentence, which is E-01's spec and the thing that keeps it from being a statistic with the inconvenient half removed.

It is allowed to say nothing, and to say "no difference"

Three outcomes, not one:

  • A difference — both sides have at least five days and the rates are far enough apart to be worth a sentence.
  • No difference — both sides qualify and the rates are close. This still gets said, and saying it matters: a surface that only speaks when something moved teaches people that silence means trouble.
  • Not yet — one side is too thin. The statement is withheld and the reason is named in plain words, rather than the surface going mysteriously blank.
The number I made up. The bank specifies "≥5 days on each side"; it says nothing about how different the two rates must be before a difference is worth stating. I used 20 percentage points. That is a designer's number with no clinical or statistical basis, it is doing real work — it decides when the app opens its mouth — and it should be reviewed by someone who knows better rather than inherited because it was in the first prototype.

The window is a chosen number too — and record D is why that matters

The ±30 minutes in E-01 is not given by anything; someone picked it. On record A the finding survives all three cuts (67% vs 18% at ±30, and still 60% vs 33% at the tighter ±15), which is what a real pattern looks like.

Record D is the same three weeks read three ways. The rough days happen to cluster at gaps of 18 to 28 minutes, and nothing else about the record changes — only the window does:

  • ±15 — withheld. The close side falls to 4 days and the gate refuses.
  • ±30 — a difference. 56% against 18%.
  • ±45 — no difference. 36% against 33%.
This is the finding, and it is about us rather than about lunch. A record can be made to say "a difference", "no difference" or "not yet" by a parameter the person never sees and we chose in an afternoon. Nobody is lying; every one of those three sentences is arithmetically true of the same twenty-one days.

Three responses, none of them comfortable. Pick a number and defend it — which requires a clinical basis we do not have. Show the sensitivity — "this holds between 20 and 45 minutes" — which is honest and is also the kind of sentence that makes a retrospective feel like a statistics package. Or only speak when the finding survives the range, which is the most conservative and would have withheld record D entirely.

I would build the third and say so in the disclosure: we only mention this when it holds however we slice it. It is the only one of the three that cannot be accused of having chosen its own evidence. It is also the one that speaks least, which is a cost we should take on purpose rather than discover.

What this found

The window question above is the headline. Everything below it is real but smaller.
E-01 depends on the quiet day, and the quiet day is scheduled for V1.

A day only enters the comparison if its afternoon is accounted for — either a rough note, or a positive mark that it was ordinary. Without the third entry state, "no rough note" means two different things, fine and unreported, and they get silently pooled. A week where someone logged doses but said nothing about how they felt would then quietly drag one side's rate down and invent a difference that is not there.

So the Explain era — the differentiator — has a hard dependency on a feature currently sitting in the V1 lane. That is not an argument for moving it; it is an argument for the Demo not pretending to do Explain.
The chart's window and the statement's window are different, and that is correct. The aligned week view shows three days; this statement is computed over the whole record. The 2026-08-26 decision already separates them: period coverage is apparatus and goes on a line under the chart, while claim coverage — "the 9 days", "the 11 days" — lives inside the sentence because it is the evidence. Two different periods on one screen is only confusing if both are called coverage.

Still open

  • What counts as "lunch". The record has three meals a day; this uses the one nearest the midday dose, which is an assumption doing quiet work. A person who eats at 11 and again at 2 breaks it.
  • Whether the two columns should be orderable. Right now rough days sit at the bottom of each column so the filled block is contiguous and countable. Sorting by date instead would let you see a bad fortnight — a different and possibly better question.
  • Whether a person can disagree with it. Every statement here is the app's read of their life. There is no affordance to say "that's not right", and there probably should be — it is also the cheapest research instrument we could build.
