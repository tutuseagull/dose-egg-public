Look Back: decided, drawn, built

An audit, not a plan. Every Look Back use case we have written down, against where it actually exists — the statement bank, a test page, or the prototype a person could open. Run 2026-09-10, before finishing the Demo.

The short answer

No, and the gap is lopsided in a way that turns out to be the useful finding.

The statement bank holds 19 sentences and the Look Back prototype speaks about five of them. That sounds like the problem. It isn't the interesting part.

The bank predates half the record. It was written when Look Back had three things in it: doses, movement, and notes. Since then we have added sleep (the domain lead called it very important, 2026-08-15), meals and protein, activity as an interval rather than a count, the slot model with add-ons (D-16), and a third entry state for quiet days. Not one of those has a single sentence in the bank. So the list isn't just under-built — it is out of date as a list, which is the more expensive kind of incomplete.
And the two halves of the differentiator have never met. The Explain era — E-01…E-05, the cross-dimension statements — is the thing nobody else in the benchmark ships, and it is 0% built. Meanwhile aligned-week-test.html now draws precisely the apparatus E-01 and E-02 need: meals, doses and rough stretches on one shared time axis. Both artefacts are ours, both are current, and neither knows the other exists. The picture has no sentence and the sentence has no picture.

2 of 6

Live: R-02 (slot spread) and the coverage-complete line. Missing: the confirmed-dose count with its named gaps (R-01), movement summary (R-03), the timing of rough notes (R-04), and the somewhere-to-be pattern (R-06). R-05 should stop being a statement.

Explain — E-01…E-05
0 of 5

The differentiator, entirely unbuilt. Every one of these needs two dimensions on the same axis — lunch against the midday dose, rough afternoons against dose lateness, dips against clock time, stiffness notes against movement days. We built that axis last week and pointed no sentence at it.

Threshold messages — T-01…T-03
2 of 3

The depth invitation (T-03 — "if you ever want to see what your afternoons have to do with the rest of your day…") is not built, and it is the one that recruits people into the Explain era.

E · Statements the new dimensions need and do not have
Sleep · Activity-as-interval · Add-ons · Quiet days · Cross-dimension
Open

Five dimensions, zero sentences. Some of these are easy ("you woke around the same time on five of the seven days"); at least one is dangerous — anything about sleep length slides toward a score, and P01's own two-part night means a "nights slept through" metric would grade her down for a strategy she chose [C-08]. That is a design problem, not a writing problem, and it should be settled before the sentences are drafted.

What the Demo actually needs

Three Look Back items sit in the Demo lane, and one of them is a suitcase word.

  • Trend views — the whole retrospective surface behind two words. This line should be decomposed before it is estimated; as written it hides everything above.
  • Two Look Back empty states — new record and lapsed record. Cheap, and one of them does not exist.
  • Sleep as its own entry type — logging is prototyped; seeing it is not.

The quiet day is V1, so a Demo Look Back may legitimately know only two entry states. Worth stating out loud rather than discovering it in the build.

The three moves I would make, in order

  1. Point one sentence at the aligned axis. Take E-01 — lunch within half an hour of the midday dose, against rough afternoons — and make aligned-week-test.html compute and say it from its own data. One statement, one chart, proving the pair works. This is the cheapest way to find out whether the differentiator is real, and it is currently the only thing on this page that is about value rather than completeness.
  2. Bring the settled decisions into the prototype. The coverage line replacing R-05, the tight container from the quiet-mark work, C-17's targets, and the slot-with-add-on mark. These are all decided and all still living in test pages — which means the prototype is quietly the oldest statement of what Look Back is.
  3. Draft the sleep statements deliberately, with the trap named first. Before writing sentences: decide what we will never say about sleep. Then two or three that describe rather than grade.
What this audit does not cover. The doctor export, appointment-anchored periods, and auto-surfaced findings are all V1 or later and were left out deliberately. Nothing here is a judgement about the quality of what is built — prototypes/look-back.html is careful work, and the coverage control with its disclosure is the strongest thing on the surface. The finding is that it stopped keeping up, not that it was wrong.
