One week, one axis

We had been designing each logging category its own picture. This is the other thing: every dimension on the same time axis, so the correlations are visible rather than asserted. One week only — the scale where detail is affordable.

Tutu, 2026-08-27. "It makes sense for each logging category to have its own visualization and summary, but what could be more interesting is to visualize all dimensions based on the same time axis, so the user can actually see the correlations… we are helping the user connect the dots." And: "let the chart tell the story" — fewer statements, not more.

The week

Tap any day. The target is the whole row; where you land sets the hour the day view would open at.

What the chart is doing

Feeling is the ground, not a lane

A rough patch is a period, not an event — so it is drawn as a soft span behind the whole day, crossing every other lane. That is the same figure/ground move we landed on for coverage, one level down, and it is what makes the correlation legible: you don't compare a "feeling row" against a "dose row", you see the dose sitting inside the rough patch or outside it.

Sleep is a band, anchored at its end

Sleep crosses midnight, so it belongs to the day you woke on. The band enters from the left edge and fades, meaning "this started before the window" — the duration is stated rather than measured off the axis, because measuring a clipped bar is a lie.

Add-ons are a treatment, not a second mark

Per D-16 a slot is one ask, so it should be one mark. An add-on shows as a ring around the dose dot. If the anchor was taken and the add-on wasn't, the ring is broken — which is a positive fact, not an absence, exactly as the entry schema requires.

The statement is secondary

One quiet line under the chart, and the coverage line under that. The chart is doing the work; the sentence only names what you are already looking at.

What I can't settle here. Three things need real data or a real person. One: whether four lanes is already too many on a 320pt phone — meals is the first candidate to drop, since protein matters to timing and timing is visible in the dose lane anyway. Two: whether the rough-span ground reads as an observation or as a verdict on the day — the same trap as the report card, one level down. Three: whether people read the vertical alignment at all without being told; the whole design assumes they scan down a time column, and that is an assumption, not a finding.

Still open

  • Activity has no lane. Walks, exercise, drumming — currently they would have to live in the meal lane as generic events, which muddles it. Either a fifth lane, or events and meals merge into one "what you did" lane with different marks.
  • What happens at six weeks. This view does not scale — twenty-eight lanes will not fit. The six-week scale keeps its own picture (the coverage grid), and the two are linked by C-17's ladder rather than being the same chart zoomed.
  • The unknown day. Wednesday here has nothing at all. It is drawn as a ruled but empty row, which is the same grammar as the coverage grid's unknown — worth checking that the two read as the same state.
