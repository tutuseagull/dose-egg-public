Chart interaction, actually touched

Open this on your phone. Two questions we were arguing about: whether a 10px dot is a usable target, and whether dragging a line is harder or easier than poking a mark. Rather than settle it in prose — try both.

Why the marks are small here. Two arguments, both Tutu's. One: people drill into a data point mostly when it looks like an outlier — and an outlier is by definition the mark with space around it. So precision matters least exactly where the clutter is worst. Two: reading a chart carefully is a good-capacity activity, not something done mid-OFF. That makes data exploration a different context of use — which is what the touch-target rule already says to size against.
Two kinds of movement, kept apart. Selection moves inside the view — which day am I looking at. Navigation moves the view itself — which week am I looking at. An earlier draft had "‹ Earlier day / Later day ›" buttons and they read as week navigation, because that is the job a pair of arrows under a chart normally does. They also pointed sideways while Option A's selection moves down the rows. Both gone. Arrows now mean weeks in both charts; selection is by touch and by arrow key only; and Clear sits next to the thing it clears.
Where the controls sit, and the one that was awkward. Period context reads before the data, so the week sits above the chart — nav, then the value readout, then the plot. The problem that had pushed it below was "Jump to this week": a control that only exists when you have navigated away, so it either shifts the layout or squats on space it does not earn. Two fixes, both cheap. One: the centre of the nav is a single fixed-height block whose second line is a status ("This week") or an action ("↩ Jump to this week") — same footprint either way, and when it is an action the whole block is the target, roughly 150 × 52px rather than a line of text. Two: with nothing selected the readout describes the week instead of sitting empty, so that row never nags and never changes height either.

Option A — day rows

Each row is a day; the dots are the times doses actually landed. The row is the target, so there is nothing small to hit. Tap any row.

The selected day survives a week change. Pick Tuesday, step back a week, and it is still Tuesday — with that week's times. That was not planned; it fell out of keeping selection as an index. It is also the two-day comparison the domain lead asked about, in embryo: same weekday, different weeks, side by side in time rather than in space. Worth raising with her.

Option B — days across, clock time up

The transpose of A: each column is a day, height is the time on the clock. Drag the line, or just tap. One line reads all three doses of that day at once.

What the scrub forced. A vertical line only means something if the x-axis means something. In the first draft of Option B the horizontal position was pure jitter — spacing to stop dots overlapping — so a scrub line would have selected nothing. Wanting the interaction is what turned x into the day. The interaction improved the chart, which is the opposite of the usual order.

It also bought something unplanned: one line now reads morning, midday and evening simultaneously. That is the comparison a person actually wants — not "what time was my midday dose on Thursday" but "what did Thursday look like."

What each form is still for

A reads the shape of a day — where doses sit inside waking hours, and how much of the day falls between them. B reads steadiness across days — a slot that holds its height is a steady slot. Same data, different question. Linked views, not competing options.

One line held. A drag may never write anything. Scrubbing is exploratory and reversible, so a missed target costs nothing — which is exactly why it is safe here and why the scroll-wheel time picker was not: that gesture landed a value on the record. Gesture cost should scale with consequence: drag to read, tap to write.

Where the 48px floor actually lands

The marks are 5px radius — nowhere near the routine floor. What is tappable is the invisible band behind each day: full-width in A, and the whole chart area in B, where a tap anywhere selects the nearest column. Both are more forgiving than a 48px button. The floor was never about the size of the ink; it is about the size of the region that responds. The WCAG 2.2 AA minimum for a target is 24px and every path here clears it several times over, with arrow keys underneath for anyone who would rather not aim at all.
